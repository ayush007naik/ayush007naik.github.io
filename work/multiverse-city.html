01 — The problem

Local commerce is fragmented.

Discovering and transacting with local businesses usually means jumping between shops, booking tools, listings and delivery apps. Each one owns a different slice of the same city.

The customer

Too many places to look

A customer who wants to shop, book an appointment and find a showtime has to use separate products with separate accounts and separate expectations.

The business

No shared workflows

Local vendors and service providers need digital storefronts, bookings and order management — but rarely have the tooling to run all of it in one place.

The thesis

One city, one surface

Make local discovery and transactions feel as easy as a single super-app, while giving businesses shared dashboards instead of disconnected tools.

03 — Key decisions

Build the ecosystem, not a demo.

The constraint was simple: one person, limited time, and a product surface wide enough for a whole city. These were the decisions that made that possible.

01

Shared backend over separate apps

Every module reads and writes to shared Supabase and Firebase projects. That keeps identity, data and state consistent while each app stays focused on its own workflow.

02

AI-assisted execution, human decisions

I use AI-assisted development to move quickly through implementation and iteration. Product scope, data models and what gets shipped are still deliberate decisions, not generated ones.

03

Ship the customer app first

The customer experience is the front door. Marketplace, appointments, real estate and entertainment went live inside it before expanding the operator surfaces.

04

Label everything honestly

Built is not the same as live. Planned is not the same as shipped. The product and this case study both use those labels so the scope is clear.

06 — Lessons

What building a city taught me.

Scope is a product decision

It is easy to design ten modules and hard to make one dependable. Shipping the customer app first forced prioritisation and made the roadmap real instead of theoretical.

Backends are where the seams show

Shared Supabase and Firebase foundations reduce duplication, but they also demand clear data ownership. The work is as much about models and permissions as it is about screens.

Speed needs a counterweight

AI-assisted workflows make implementation fast. The counterweight is verification — checking flows, links and claims before calling anything shipped.

Credibility compounds

Clearly separating live, built and planned work is not just honest — it makes the product easier to explain to users, collaborators and anyone evaluating the engineering.
